Product engineering review · 6 May 2026

Zonna's AI is doing the work, but hiding the receipts.

Six surfaces in the app are LLM-generated. Five wear a 9px sparkle and a four-letter pill. Two don't wear anything at all. For paid features that cost real money on the API and real money on the user's card, the provenance is too quiet — restraint has crossed into invisibility.

2 · The verdict, after 20 years of shipping

The current AICoachChip is well-engineered — a single source of truth, honest provenance rules, working-state pulse instead of a spinner. That part is right. What's wrong is the scale of it. A 9-pixel sparkle next to a 4-letter brand name (Kit) at 11px font weight, hidden above an eyebrow label, will not register on a phone in sunlight after a run. It registers as decoration.

Two compounding problems make it worse:

  • The chip is a label, not a byline. "Kit" is a name, but there's no person attached. It reads as a category tag, not as authorship. Users don't think "Kit wrote this" — they think "this is the Kit section".
  • The two highest-value AI surfaces aren't marked at all. Run feedback (every session) and plan adjustments (the one thing competitors can't replicate) — the moments where a user might think "this is why I pay" — are the moments with no provenance.

Memory note from your past sessions: "bias high on visibility for value-add UI; default louder than feels right." That instinct is correct. We are currently calibrated for the design awards, not the user.

3 · Three recommendations, ranked by leverage

Recommendation 1

Promote the chip to a coach byline.

Replace the 11px pill with a proper byline: 22px Kit avatar, name on top, role on bottom ("KIT · YOUR COACH"), a small live sparkle anchored to the avatar. Same restraint, but now reads as authored content, not a tag. The avatar gives provenance a face — the same trick Granola, Notion AI and Superhuman use.

Tradeoff: Costs ~16px of vertical card space. Pays for itself by making the AI feature legible from arm's length.

Add a 3px moss left-rail to AI cards.

Recommendation 2

A thin vertical accent down the left edge of every card whose body is model-generated. Same colour token as the avatar (moss / warn). Cheap to render, instantly telegraphs "this card is coached, the next one isn't". Linear and Arc both use this for AI-modified content. It also replaces the "!" warning eyebrow on adjustments — they're advice, not alerts.

Tradeoff: Adds another structural rule to enforce. Worth it: the rail is the cheapest-possible signal that scales to every future AI surface without redesign.
Recommendation 3

Mark the two unmarked surfaces, today.

Run feedback paragraph and plan adjustments are both LLM output and both currently anonymous. Wrap each in a CoachNoteBlock-equivalent with the new byline. Run feedback gets a moss byline (post-session read). Plan adjustments get a warn byline (decision needed) — but as coach voice, not as system alert. The "!" circle goes.

Tradeoff: Two component changes, ~1 day. Highest-impact visibility win in the codebase right now.

6 · Cost to ship

Three component edits: AICoachChip (replace pill with byline), CoachNoteBlock (add the moss/warn left-rail), RunFeedbackCard & PendingAdjustmentBanner (wrap the LLM paragraph / replace the "!" eyebrow).

One new shared primitive: CoachByline — avatar + name + role + optional working state. Lives next to AICoachChip.tsx. AICoachChip can stay deprecated-but-aliased so existing call sites don't break the build.

Estimate: 1 engineer-day for the components, 0.5 day for the rollout across the six surfaces, 0.5 day for the regression sweep. Two days end-to-end, one TestFlight build, and the AI features finally look like the thing the user is paying for.
