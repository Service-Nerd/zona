# Strength & conditioning for runners — research brief (2026-10-16)

**For:** `STRENGTH-ENGINE-01` (the separate, PAID strength engine). **Method:** online literature and product
research, sources linked inline. Several figures come from abstracts (Europe PMC) because full texts were
gated; that is marked. **This file is evidence, not doctrine.** Doctrine lands in `StrengthPrinciples.md`
through the Coaching Board.

## Findings that shape the product

1. **Heavy (or heavy + plyometric) work is what improves running economy** (~2–8% in most studies; Blagrove
   2018). Submaximal and isometric loads showed **no** significant RE gain (Llanos-Lagos 2024). Plyometrics
   helped RE **only at ≤12 km/h** — the slower, recreational end, i.e. Zonna's runners.
2. **Bodyweight-only cannot reach the "heavy" stimulus.** Bands produce strength gains similar to conventional
   training ([Lopes 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6383082/)), but a bodyweight tier has a real
   ceiling, **and the app must say so**.
3. 🔴 **No injury-prevention claim.** Lauersen 2014/2018 (RR ~0.32) is mostly team-sport/military. The only
   runner meta-analysis (Wu, …, Blagrove, *Sports Med* 2024, 9 RCTs, n=1,904) found exercise programmes **did
   not** significantly reduce running injury; only **supervised** ones did, post hoc. An unsupervised app making
   that claim is a Coaching Board matter (the W-03 precedent).
4. **Interference is about timing, not existence.** Concurrent training does not blunt maximal strength
   (Schumann 2022); explosive gains are blunted only within the same session; a 6RM leg session impaired
   submaximal running for ~48 h in untrained men (Doma). Rule of thumb, consistent across sources and Runna's
   own guidance: run first, lift later (≥3–6 h), or lift on an easy day; keep heavy legs away from intervals and
   long runs; upper body almost anywhere.
5. **"Running-focused" vs "general" is a real distinction**, in load type and fatigue management. Running-focused:
   heavy lower body, calf/soleus, single-leg, hip abductors, anti-rotation core, plyometrics, low volume.
   General: adds upper body and hypertrophy volume. Weight gain is a weak concern (body composition unchanged in
   Blagrove, Beattie, Piacentini).
6. **Women, peri/post-menopause:** LIFTMOR (supervised, 5×5 >85% 1RM + impact) raised lumbar BMD 2.9% vs a 1.2%
   loss in controls ([JBMR](https://academic.oup.com/jbmr/article-abstract/33/2/211/7605709)). UK consensus
   "Strong, Steady and Straight" (BJSM 2022, Royal Osteoporosis Society) supports progressive resistance + moderate
   impact **unless** vertebral / multiple low-trauma fracture ([BJSM](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/56/15/837)).
7. **Injury-history variants, not rehab.** Patellofemoral: hip + knee exercise combined (2016/2018 consensus).
   Achilles: tendon loading first-line, ≥3×/wk unless frail (JOSPT 2024). IT band: hip-abductor link is mixed.
   Shin: no strength-prescription evidence.
8. **Warm-up:** RAMP (Raise, Activate, Mobilise, Potentiate; UKSCA-recommended). Specific ramp sets before the
   first heavy lift are the most consistently useful part. ~8–10 min.

## Safety copy the product must carry

- Pre-participation screening per ACSM 2015 (activity level, known cardiovascular/metabolic/renal disease or
  symptoms, intended intensity).
- Pain-monitoring model: 0–2/10 fine, 2–5 acceptable, >5 stop; should settle by next morning (thresholds vary).
- Diagnosed osteoporosis or vertebral fracture: professional guidance before impact work or loaded spinal flexion.

## Competitors

- **Runna** (Strava, since Apr 2025): Running Focus or All Round; Beginner/Intermediate/Advanced; **30/45/60
  min**, up to 4/wk; equipment checklist (band, barbell, box, bench, DB, KB, pull-up bar, Swiss ball); tap an
  exercise for an **animation and form tips**; reps and weight logged per set. 🔑 **Strength sits OUTSIDE its
  adaptive plan and does not affect adaptation** ([Runna support](https://support.runna.com/en/articles/15624879-adding-strength-training-to-your-runna-plan)).
  That is the gap a strength engine that genuinely works WITH the run plan fills.
- **Garmin Coach:** strength plans with equipment choice, reported free. **Nike Training Club:** free video
  workouts. **Fitbod:** algorithmic, ~1,600 video demos, ~$15.99/mo.

## Board-seat candidates (modelled on published positions only; never implying endorsement)

1. **Richard Blagrove** — Loughborough; UKSCA-accredited; author of *Strength and Conditioning for Endurance
   Running* (2015); the 2018 systematic review; co-author of the 2024 meta-analysis that found unsupervised
   prevention programmes do not reduce running injury. UK-based. **Strongest fit.**
2. **Bent Rønnestad** — heavy/explosive strength for endurance (with Mujika, 2014). Leans elite and cycling.
3. **Kenji Doma** — residual fatigue and scheduling (lower-body RT impairs running at 24–48 h). Verify the
   Schumann 2022 co-authorship before citing.

## Where evidence is weak

Injury prevention in runners (null in the runner meta-analysis) · plyometric dose (convention) · taper / deload
strength handling (one n=8 study) · strength vs bone stress injury in runners (mechanistic) · masters and
peri-menopausal runner trials (small or secondary) · the 24–48 h rule (untrained men) · RIR for runners (none) ·
bodyweight-only RE gains (not shown) · the 2026 ACSM stand's numbers (not retrieved).
