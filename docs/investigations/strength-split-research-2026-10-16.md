# Strength split, frequency, core and minimum dose — research brief (2026-10-16)

**For:** `STRENGTH-ENGINE-01`, ADR-024 §6a. **Evidence, not doctrine.** Primary sources read: ACSM 2026 stand
(Currier et al., [PMC12965823](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823)), Blagrove 2018 (partial), Iversen
2021. Ramos-Campo 2024 and Pelland's final paper were **not** retrieved (secondary / preprint only): verify before
treating as doctrine.

## Key numbers

| Question | Number | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minimum frequency | Every major muscle group **≥2×/wk** | Position stand | ACSM 2026 |
| Full body vs split, volume equated | **No meaningful difference** (strength, size) | Meta-analysis, 14 studies (secondary) | Ramos-Campo 2024 |
| Frequency, volume equated: size | No significant effect | Meta-analysis, 25 studies | [Schoenfeld 2019](https://vuir.vu.edu.au/38370/) |
| Frequency, volume equated: strength | None (Ralston) / small slope ~3.3% per extra day (Pelland preprint) | Contested | [Ralston 2018](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1186/s40798-018-0149-9) · [Pelland](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/460) |
| Minimum weekly sets for gains | **≥4 per muscle group** | Narrative review | [Iversen 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8449772) |
| Strength volume per exercise | Plateau ~**2–3 sets** | Position stand | ACSM 2026 |
| Maintenance | **1 session/wk, 1 set per exercise**, intensity kept, up to 32 wk; older adults ~2 sessions, 2–3 sets | Narrative review | [Spiering 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33629972/) |
| Runner strength frequency | 2–3/wk | Systematic review, 24 studies | [Blagrove 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5889786/) |
| Interference | Lower-body only, males (SMD −0.43), none in females | Meta-analysis, 59 studies | [Huiberts 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37847373/) |

## Decision rule (synthesis; no source keys a rule to 1–4 days)

1 day → full body (maintenance, below the ACSM floor) · 2 → full body × 2 (only way to hit each region twice; ACSM
2009 novice guidance) · 3 → full body × 2 + one lighter day (ACSM 2009 intermediate 3–4/wk) · 4 → upper/lower × 2
(NSCA's intermediate example, [NSCA](https://www.nsca.com/education/articles/kinetic-select/determination-of-resistance-training-frequency/)).
Volume and intensity drive results; structure is about fit and adherence (ACSM 2026: the best programme is the one
kept). Supersets roughly halve session time (Iversen).

## Fit to the run week

Interference is a **lower-body** problem (Huiberts; Schumann 2022: maximal strength not blunted, explosive may be;
running interferes more than cycling, Wilson 2012). So lower days go after hard runs or ≥48 h before a key session;
upper days can go anywhere. **No study tests this scheduling directly**; it is inference.

## Core

No review compares trunk-training frequencies; none in runners. Balance meta-analyses associate >2 sessions/wk with
larger gains at low certainty. **Defensible position:** ≥2 exposures/wk; a short block in most sessions is a
practical delivery, not an evidence requirement.

## Where evidence is weak

Ramos-Campo and Pelland unverified · frequency-for-strength contested · split ↔ run-week alignment untested ·
Doma's samples small · core frequency has no direct trials · maintenance data general-population, not runners ·
ACSM 2026 calls minimal-dose and concurrent evidence insufficient · few female studies in the interference data.
