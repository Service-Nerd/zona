## Evidence

Running minutes per week, three 30-minute sessions, each a 5-minute walk warm-up (`RUNWALK_WARMUP_WALK_MINS`, §117
addendum) and a 25-minute block of run/walk repeats starting on a run:

| Ladder | 1/2 | 2/2 | 3/2 | 4/x | 5/x | 6/1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** (walk stays 2, drops to 1 last) | 27 | 39 (+44%) | 45 (+15%) | 51 (+13%) | 57 (+12%) | 66 (+16%) |
| **B** (walk drops to 1 at 4) | 27 | 39 (+44%) | 45 (+15%) | **60 (+33%)** | 63 (+5%) | 66 (+5%) |

At 6/1 the runner covers **6.0 to 8.2 km of running a week** at 11 to 8 min/km. A 0 km/week marathoner 26 weeks out:
the walk-run start (7 weeks, one held deload) + §116's ramp 6 to 12 km (9 weeks) + §44's block (10) = **26**, exactly.
The live 0 km/week runner was 26 to 29 weeks out.

`measure:fitness` and the envelope do not reach this cohort (neither grid has a start below 6 km/week that stages);
that is recorded, not waived. The first measurement of it is Phase E.

## The board

**🏃 Hutchinson (chair).** None of these five is new physiology; they are the decisions a coach makes on a whiteboard
before the first session, and the endpoints are already ruled. Rule the ones the evidence settles, and say plainly
which rest on judgement.

**📊 Seiler.** All easy, as Am. 6 requires. No objection.

**🩹 Willy.** L1: ladder A. The walk is the recovery between impacts, and shortening it is the larger tissue change,
so it happens once, last, onto §117's already-ratified pattern. B puts a +33% step mid-ladder for no reason. L5: the
transition worries me more than the ladder. 6/1 means no more than six minutes of continuous running. The §116 ramp at
6 km over three days is two-kilometre continuous runs, about seventeen minutes. That is a near-tripling of the
continuous bout in one week, which is the per-run load event Am. 2 exists for. **So the ramp after a walk-run start
keeps §117's 6/1 pattern on every session until the checkpoint**: total minutes unchanged, walk breaks kept. §117 Am. 4
already separates the prescription from the peak, so this is that prescription applied, not a new one.

**⚕️ Sims.** Three days, never two in a row where the days allow: recovery between impact days is the point at this
stage. The 30 minutes is time on feet, warm-up included; a beginner returning after years off should finish wanting
more. Fuelling note carried. On L2, everyone below 6 starts at 1/2: we do not know they can run continuously, and the
first weeks are cheap.

**🎯 McMillan.** L2: I disagree with Sims. A runner doing 5 km a week spends two weeks below what they already do, on a
runway that is often tight. But the alternative is converting kilometres to a ladder level with a pace we do not have
for these runners, which is a guess wearing a number. I will take 1/2 for everyone if the walk-run start is never
shortened to fit a runway and the cost shows up in Phase E's measurement. L4: when the runway cannot hold the walk-run
start plus §44's block, they still get the walk-run start with the race on it. Never a refusal at signup (Design
amendment 1); the checkpoint then offers what builds, which is C4.

**💪 Blagrove.** Silent.
