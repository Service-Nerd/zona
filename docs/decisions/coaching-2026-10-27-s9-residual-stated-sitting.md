# Coaching Board: does a §9 cut have to say what it cost? (2026-10-27)

**Trigger:** soft. Q1 is what the plan *says* (§40c is a prescription-honesty rule); Q2 would change prescription if
the answer were "smooth".
**Evidence:** `docs/decisions/coaching-2026-10-27-s9-residual-stated.md` (read in full).
**`measure:fitness`:** not run, deliberately. The question is whether to add a note, which moves no session. Option
"smooth" was refused below, so nothing prescribes differently.

## Conflict scan (sections and amendments)

- **§40c:** scoped to a *life-first constraint*, threshold 10% of the intended peak week, the note names a lever. A §9
  cut fails all three tests: not a life constraint, under the threshold (worst 9.6%), and no runner-owned lever.
- **§34 / ADR-022 (declared residuals):** a residual is declared when a rule is broken. Here §9 is kept.
- **§90:** binds "told easier ⇒ delivers less". It says nothing about a lighter week that is not labelled.
- **S9-RATIO-MINUTES-01 (Willy, binding):** smoothing would mean moving the cut minutes somewhere, which for the long
  run that ruling forbids, and for other easy runs re-breaks §9 the same week.
- **§3 / §47:** deloads and step-backs are labelled because they are planned. A dip from a clamp is not a planned
  recovery and must not be labelled as one.
- **§1:** no effect.

## The seats

🏃 **Hutchinson (chair).** §40c exists because a 45-minute weekday cap cost runners a quarter of their peak and they
were never told. This is not that. The worst plan loses 9.6% of its peak week and the median is a few minutes, under the
line the board drew precisely so notes would mean something. Stating it would be the "notes that fire on noise" failure,
in our own words.

📊 **Seiler.** Nothing here changes the distribution. A week 6–13% lighter in the middle of a build is inside the
week-to-week variation every real athlete's log shows. I would not want the plan to call it anything.

🎯 **McMillan.** A note needs a lever, and there isn't one: the runner did nothing to cause this and can do nothing to
change it. On the 51 dips, a runner who sees 221 between 236 and 268 does not need an explanation; they need the next
week to go up, and it does. Leave them.

🩹 **Willy.** From the load seat, a dip is not a risk. Every one of the 51 is followed by the build resuming, and none
exceeds the ramp. Smoothing them is the option I would refuse: the only places the minutes can go are the long run,
which my condition closed, or an easy run already at its §9 ceiling. Leave the dips.

⚕️ **Sims.** No objection. If anything, an occasional lighter week in a build is kind to the runners I worry about.

💪 **Blagrove.** No objection from this seat.

## Ruling

| Item | Ruling |
|---|---|
| **Q1, a note for a §9 cut** | **INCORRECT.** §40c does not reach it: not a life-first constraint, below its 10% peak-week threshold (worst 9.6% km, 6.0% minutes), and no lever to name. A note here would fire on noise. No note. |
| **Q2, the 51 mid-build dips** | **Leave them. Labelling: INCORRECT** (a clamp is not a planned recovery; §3/§47 labels are for planned weeks). **Smoothing: INCORRECT** (it re-breaks §9 or moves minutes into the long run, against S9-RATIO-MINUTES-01). |
| **The item** | Closed as ruled. Nothing to build. |
